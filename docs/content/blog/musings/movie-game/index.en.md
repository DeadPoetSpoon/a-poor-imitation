# The Artistic Sense of Film

Film can be called the seventh art, but what exactly gives film its unique "artistic sense"? And what distinguishes it from other art forms?

Script? Music? Performance? Cinematography? Mise-en-scène? Editing? Audiovisual language?

# The Cinematic Quality of Games

The composition of games is remarkably similar to that of films — the biggest difference is perhaps the "sense of control" unique to games.

## Excellent Game Recordings

We can start from excellent game recordings. When watching a game recording, the audience actually loses the "control" over the game, which makes the game infinitely closer to film. This gives the audience, or rather the players, an intuitive film-like artistic sense, prompting comments like "At this point, it has become art" in the bullet chat.

## Story-driven Games

If we add a little "sense of control," "game recordings" transform into game novels, single-path story games, multi-path story games, and other game genres — such as *The Invisible Guardian*, *Titanfall*, *Detroit: Become Human*, etc. The source of artistic sense in such games is perhaps not far from that of film; they can be viewed as enumerations of countless "game recordings."

## Simulation Games

If we maximize the "sense of control," we enter the realm of simulation games. They construct a rule-based or randomized world for players to freely explore or act within — such as *Dwarf Fortress*, *Red Dead Redemption*, *Minecraft*, *Euro Truck Simulator*, etc. In the hands of skilled players, such games can become "artistic" game recordings, while for average players, the artistic sense may not be as immediate as in story-driven games.

# The Artistic Sense in Creation

I believe the artistic sense of games should be the artistic sense of "creating art." With a book, a piece of music, or a film, as readers, listeners, or viewers, we are all passive recipients. But games allow players to participate in the creation of art. Other media can be re-created, but firsthand smoke is still better than secondhand. This sense of "creating art" is perhaps the "artistic sense" that distinguishes games from traditional arts.

# Artistic Sense Needs a Theme

Artistic sense needs a theme. In *Dwarf Fortress*, merely surviving is hard to turn into art, but add those random events, add the player's care and concern for each dwarf, and you have the artistic sense in creation. In *Red Dead Redemption*, merely completing missions is hard to turn into art, but the player's repeated free exploration and decisions to enter that cowboy's world create the artistic sense in creation. In *Minecraft*, merely mining and fighting the dragon is hard to turn into art, but add a hundred-player survival experiment, and you have the artistic sense in creation. In *Euro Truck Simulator*, merely driving is hard to turn into art, but add the intersection of a busy urban life with the simple, repetitive appreciation of roadside scenery, and you have the artistic sense in creation.
