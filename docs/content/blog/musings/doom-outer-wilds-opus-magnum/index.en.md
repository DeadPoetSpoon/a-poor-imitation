# DOOM · Outer Wilds · Opus Magnum

Putting these three games side by side is my way of discussing the balance between body and mind in gameplay. To put it more abstractly: the ratio of feeling to thinking. "Body" — the feeling — means surrendering yourself entirely to the game, sinking into its rhythm. "Mind" — the output — means understanding the game's logic, participating in it, and testing your own ideas. This distinction also hints at where a game's sense of "pleasure" comes from: finishing a battle or solving a puzzle? Experiencing a story or conquering the final boss?

# DOOM

DOOM is the gold standard of "pure combat bliss." When I first played DOOM, its extreme glory-kill animations left a deep impression on me — the classic shotgun in its final form shredding the demons before me, bang bang, a few shots, and the glory kills on those elites only got more mind-blowing to watch. Later, DOOM: The Dark Ages added parrying, elevating the combat bliss to a whole new level, and then there's the mech combat where every punch lands with weight. To quote a fellow player's comment: "It just smooths out the wrinkles in your brain." Don't ask me about the story — all I know is that when the music kicks in, I'm going to shred everything in front of me that pisses me off! But this actually fits DOOM's character perfectly. And this exhilarating gameplay experience expresses the most primal purpose of games in the most direct way: "pleasure."

# Outer Wilds

I love Outer Wilds. I think it achieves a certain balance between body and mind. Drifting through the vastness of space, every planet simulated in real time, each one a brand-new experience. And as you travel between the stars, gradually piecing together fragments of memory, those lives that were once vivid and the lives that are vivid right now are accomplishing something grand together: endless exploration, and the courage to set out once again. Even if you follow a guide and skip every mystery (which almost never happens), you'll still get a beautiful story. Its puzzles are excellent too — those "Eureka moments" I still wish I could experience again. A piece of information you stumble upon on one planet might well be the key clue for another. This fragmented, disconnected knowledge piles up layer by layer until it suddenly erupts at some moment. That pleasure belongs to the mind. So what if the universe explodes? I was there.

# Opus Magnum

Opus Magnum is a purely puzzle game I've played — pure in the best way. Simple, clear mechanics: just click-click-click with the mouse and you're done. Your body gets almost no feedback. There's no real story, just puzzle after puzzle, each more complex than the last, waiting to be solved. This experience reminds me of the joy of playing Sudoku on a Nokia phone as a kid. One thing I really appreciate about Opus Magnum: its solutions are diverse. You can optimize for efficiency, for cost, for elegance — you can even fill the entire screen with mechanical arms simply because you want to. Even so, you can still beat the level. This "leniency" gives the mind tremendous room to play and makes space for the sense of "pleasure."

# Closing Thoughts

I can't pick my favorite ratio between the two. But after a long day of work, I love the catharsis of DOOM; on short holidays, I enjoy the romantic exploration of Outer Wilds; on long vacations, I'd rather exercise my brain tinkering with the mechanical arms of Opus Magnum. Games always manage to keep me company with joy at every moment — and that, too, is one of the great charms of gaming!
