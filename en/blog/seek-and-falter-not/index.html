Seek, and Falter Not

To strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield.

The Lost Two Years

It's been almost three years since the API project began, and two years since the last update. My life has changed so much in these two years — job changes, life in chaos...

In 2024, I took out a loan and bought a house. I thought everything was heading in the right direction — my biggest material desire had been fulfilled, and it was time to satisfy my spiritual desires. I believed the API project could gradually get on track. But then my once-stable job began to falter. A tidal wave called "the bad economy" crashed through the already fragile levee. Salaries started coming late. For months, staring at a bank balance of a few thousand yuan, I lay in bed in the middle of the night wondering if I had made the right choices. I threw myself into work because when basic survival isn't met, a person cannot think, cannot create.

In 2025: pay cuts, job insecurity, leadership changes. Months of just a basic salary made me truly feel what it means to "live beyond one's means." One day, lying on the hard bed of a rental room on a business trip, I stared at the ceiling reflecting the headlights of passing traffic outside the window. For the first time, I was afraid to fall asleep — because the next day, I'd have to face it all over again. Along with these blows came the "rules" hidden beneath a veneer of "thriving prosperity." You must accept "being exploited and exploiting" just to get a "ticket" to enter the game. Every desire you have becomes a tool to be used against you, and you must personally hand the "whip" to the one who wields it. With every lash, you must loudly shout, "Work hard! Strive!" — without a single complaint. This is a kind of alienation — the alienation of money and power.

In 2026, things have stabilized a little. Looking back on these years, I've come to understand an immense sense of "depletion." For two years, life has been muddled. My weight shot from 67 kg to 80 kg. At one point, I lost all passion for work and life, trapped in a conflicted, tangled state: on one hand, unwilling to be just a beast of burden but also unwilling to buy a "ticket" (whether to enter or exit the game); on the other, wanting to settle into life but unwilling to abandon all the effort I'd already poured in. "Accepting the rules" is clearly the simplest path, yet I kept questioning why I couldn't just keep walking it, forcing myself to change while simultaneously denying that I had the ability to change.

"You're bullying yourself, you know." — We Are Made of Miracles (a Japanese drama I recently stumbled upon)

The Last Straw

One day, a wave of exhaustion washed over me, and I couldn't help but mutter to my parents, "I'm mentally drained." I almost never do this, because it always ends embarrassingly. For that reason, I've been unwilling to bring up any of it: my poor finances, the internal friction from work changes... mentioning it only tires me out more. Maybe, as someone once said, I'm just not good at communicating.

"Your mom told me you're feeling very mentally tired lately, so I wanted to talk with you, have a heart-to-heart 😁"

"Honestly, after doing the same job for many years, anyone would feel fed up — things like scheming between colleagues, being deceived by leaders, the crushing weight of work, and so on."

"The key is learning to adjust your mindset, and mastering how to communicate with colleagues and leaders. I remember telling you before: don't compete with leaders for credit, don't compete with colleagues for favor, don't compete with subordinates for profit."

"Young people all have their own personalities, but you shouldn't show it too openly — just let people sense it. Especially in front of leaders: even if you have a different opinion, don't bring it up directly in front of colleagues. If you must speak, do it tactfully, or discuss it with them privately. Being humble and obedient before a leader is not shameful."

"I suggest you look up Reader's Digest collections on Douyin, download them, and read them carefully. They'll really help with your people skills and your ideological development."

"And love won't just come knocking while you sit at home. It comes through your interactions and communication with leaders and colleagues. Understand?"

My father tried earnestly to counsel me. I'm grateful to him, and grateful he helped me fulfill my desire to buy a home. It's just that I read countless copies of Reader's Digest back in middle and high school already; in today's world, marriage doesn't necessarily make life better; and a humble, kind person absolutely cannot smoothly climb the ladder.

Once again, I've deeply realized: so many things are inner conflicts, and inner conflicts can only be resolved by oneself. I understood — I can't go on like this anymore!

"I've never been good at getting along with others. But I've already learned to get along well with the person I most wanted to. That's enough. That person is myself." — We Are Made of Miracles

The Tortoise or the Hare

"Why didn't the tortoise wake up the hare?" — We Are Made of Miracles

Throughout this period, books have kept me company as they always do. After finishing Madame Bovary, I fell in love with Flaubert's exquisite metaphors and came to learn about his pure, devoted life. Before the words he poured his entire life into, even through the lens of translation, I felt ashamed: even if I had his material conditions, I might not have his determination and perseverance to devote a lifetime to what one loves — or maybe I simply lack that ability. The pressure of "society" forces people to turn from tortoises into hares.

And so, once again, I revisited the expectations I had for my life when I graduated from university:

Id: A life of low desire, stability, and tranquility.

Ego: 8 hours of work, 8 hours of sleep, 8 hours left for books, film, and games.

Superego: To serve the public good.

I've done poorly at all three. My life hasn't reached a stable state; work hours far exceed eight; sleep is severely insufficient; much of the remaining time is wasted on internal friction and meaningless distractions; and my ideal of serving the public good hasn't been well protected — it's been alienated into a pursuit of "success."

I need to be more confident. I should be the "tortoise"! Or rather, I need myself to become the "tortoise"!

Setting Out Again

"When you focus on what's right in front of you, your wishes come true." — We Are Made of Miracles

I remembered a short line I wrote almost ten years ago:

I will die here, but tomorrow I will return.

I believe I have the courage to set out again!

"What abandoned me yesterday cannot be retained; what troubles my heart today brings endless sorrow." — Li Bai

Seek, and falter not.

To strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield. — Ulysses

Why not start by listing 100 of my own strengths?
